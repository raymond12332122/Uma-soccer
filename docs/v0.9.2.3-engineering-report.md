# v0.9.2.3 — Final visual artifact + possession regression

Two blockers were investigated. They did not come out the same way, and this
report says so plainly:

- **The artifact is found, named, and fixed.** It is the **goal nets**, and it
  is neither the stands nor the camera clipping through anything. The previous
  pass's explanation was incomplete exactly as QA said.
- **The steal regression is not reproducible in this build.** Every path was
  traced and measured; nothing acquires the ball from outside the established
  geometry. That is a negative result, and it is reported as one rather than
  dressed up as a fix.

## 1. The artifact, by object identity

### The measurement that was wrong first

`tests/diag_camera_intrusion.gd` watches a live match and, every rendered
frame, tests every drawable against the camera. Its first run flagged all 22
characters as ~130 m boxes swallowing the camera:

```
world aabb pos=(-33.64, -0.16, -57.39) size=(43.50, 134.76, 128.23)
camera=(9.94, 7.50, 8.99) dist=63.63 surface 0.080 m from the camera
```

That was **my measurement error, not a defect.** `tests/diag_render_bounds.gd`
settles it directly:

```
get_aabb()  size  (16.56, 18.38, 5.73)   -> in world (124.65, 138.16, 43.44)
custom_aabb size  (0.29,  0.33,  0.17)   -> in world (  2.16,   2.51,  1.30)
get_aabb() == custom_aabb ? false
posed bone extent in WORLD (0.58, 1.37, 0.75), characters 1.37–1.55 m tall
```

`VisualInstance3D.get_aabb()` returns the raw mesh bounds and **ignores
`custom_aabb` entirely**. The giant number is the *input* to the v0.9.2.1
correction, not anything the renderer sees. The corrected bounds are 2.16 ×
2.51 × 1.30 m around a 1.55 m character — sane, and the v0.9.2.1 fix is intact.
The watcher now reads the effective bounds; re-run over 3600 frames it reports
**zero** INSIDE, NEAR or HUGE events.

### What the artifact actually is

Captured from the real gameplay camera during a live match, at camera
(15.2, 13.1) — a large pale angular structure entering from the right with hard
dark bars radiating across the pitch, which is precisely the v0.9.2.2
recording's description.

`tests/diag_goal_artifact.gd` freezes that exact pose and re-renders it,
changing one thing at a time. It measures **pale structure** (bright and
desaturated — which the pitch never is) and **shadowed pitch** in the region
the artifact occupies, rather than a frame-wide dark fraction:

```
                            shadowed-pitch   pale-structure
00_baseline                       2.21%           7.50%
01_goal_frame_hidden              1.96%           7.21%   <- posts are thin
02_goal_nets_hidden               4.61%           2.97%   <- 60% of the slab
03_net_shadows_off                2.31%           7.60%   <- nets cast nothing
04_all_goal_shadows_off           1.66%           7.38%
05_directional_shadow_off         0.16%           8.83%
```

**The pale slab is the four translucent net panels per goal.** The dark bars
are ordinary directional shadow, not a goal or net shadow defect — turning the
nets' shadow casting off changes nothing (2.21 → 2.31), and the goal's whole
shadow accounts for only 0.55 of the 2.21.

### Why v0.9.2.2 cleared the goals

v0.9.2.2's isolation sweep measured **whole-frame dark fraction** and recorded
`03_goals_hidden 0.1472` against a `00_baseline 0.1454`, concluding "goals are
not it". That metric is blind to this artifact in both directions: the slab is
*bright*, and the shadow is *localised*. Both vanish into a frame-wide average.
This is exactly what the brief meant by "do not use only dark fraction" and
"we need object identity", and it is why the stands explanation was incomplete
rather than wrong — the ambient fix was real and did remove the black stands;
this was the other thing in the same frames.

### Root cause

`scenes/Field.tscn`, the net material:

```
albedo_color = Color(1, 1, 1, 0.32)
transparency = 1
cull_mode = 2          # CULL_DISABLED
shading_mode = 0       # UNSHADED
```

The net panels are **closed boxes** (`BoxMesh` 0.06 m thick). `CULL_DISABLED`
on a closed box draws the front *and* back face of every panel — the same
surface twice — for no visual benefit whatsoever, because a box already has a
face pointing at you from either side. A view ray through a goal crosses 2–3
panels, so alpha 0.32 was compositing over 4–6 layers:

```
1 - 0.68^5  =  0.855      near-opaque white
```

which is the "large white/grey angular slab". With culling restored it is 2–3
layers:

```
1 - 0.68^3  =  0.686      translucent; the pitch shows through
```

### Fix, and the measurement of it

One flag removed — `cull_mode` back to its default (back-face culling):

```
                            pale-structure   net's own contribution
before (CULL_DISABLED)          7.50%             4.53 points
after  (back-face culling)      5.21%             2.29 points
```

The net's contribution **halved**, exactly as the layer count predicts, and the
rendered frame now shows the grass and the goal-frame shadow *through* the net
instead of a solid white panel. Nothing was hidden and no geometry was removed:
the goal, its nets and all thirteen goal meshes still render and still hold
their collision bodies.

## 2. Object-level isolation instrumentation

`tests/diag_camera_intrusion.gd` (live match) flags, per frame, any drawable
whose effective bounds contain the camera, come within 0.35 m of it, cover more
than 55% of the frame by **measured ray-grid coverage**, move more than 6 m
between frames, change size by more than 2.5×, or contain a non-finite value.
Every flag prints node path, owner group, world transform, world AABB, camera
distance, and — for a character — the animation playing and the skeleton's own
bone span.

No debug material or debug ID was ever assigned to scene geometry, and none
ships. The identification was done by measured coverage and by hiding groups in
a diagnostic scene, both of which leave the shipped scene untouched.

## 3. Is the camera entering geometry? No — measured, not argued

A 60-second match is a sample, not a proof: the first live run's camera only
reached x 1.85..17.81, and the goals are at x ±29, so it never visited the
poses in question. `tests/diag_camera_sweep.gd` therefore drives the rig
through **all 510 reachable poses** (x −33..33, z −21..21 — the ball can sit in
the back of a net at x ±32.2, and `CameraController` clamps nothing) with the
real camera node, offsets and FOV:

```
objects the camera was ever INSIDE:                     0
objects that ever came within 0.35 m of the camera:     0

largest measured screen coverage, per object:
  100.0%  Ground            (100.0, 0.0, 70.0)   at 21.0 m
   26.0%  StandEastEnd        (3.0, 5.0, 40.0)   at  6.1 m, camera ( 33.0, -3.1)
   26.0%  StandWestEnd        (3.0, 5.0, 40.0)   at  6.1 m, camera (-33.0, -3.1)
   22.2%  GoalRightNetTop     (3.2, 0.1,  8.0)   at  4.9 m, camera ( 29.0,  2.9)
   22.2%  GoalLeftNetTop      (3.2, 0.1,  8.0)   at  5.2 m, camera (-33.0,  2.9)
   12.5%  goal net back       (0.1, 3.0,  8.0)   at  7.8 m
```

And over the 3600-frame live match, closest camera approach to any stadium
mesh:

```
GoalRightSideBarB   12.18 m      StandSouthTier1     18.29 m
GoalRightNetTop     12.21 m      StandEastEnd        18.86 m
GoalRightBackBar    15.06 m      StandNorthTier1     33.17 m
```

**The camera never enters, touches, or near-plane-clips any geometry.** The
near plane (0.05 m), the follow offsets and the smoothing are all exonerated by
measurement. The intrusion is an object rendering as an opaque slab at 5–12 m,
not a clipping failure — which is why the fix is the material and not the
camera.

## 4. Is animation involved? No — and here is the proof

- **Bounds:** corrected `custom_aabb` is 2.16 × 2.51 × 1.30 m in world space
  around a 1.55 m character (section 1). No 190 m volumes reach the renderer.
- **Skeletons at flagged frames:** every character flag printed its bone span.
  Measured spans were 0.05–0.21 in bone units across the whole run — no bone
  explosion, no limb leaving its envelope.
- **Non-finite values:** the NAN check fired zero times over 3600 frames on 291
  drawables.
- **One-frame scale spikes:** the SCALE check (>2.5× in one frame) fired zero
  times.
- **Discontinuities:** 126 JUMP events, all of them a character's meshes moving
  together when a player respawns at kickoff — expected, and never correlated
  with a rendered artifact.
- **Which clip was playing** at each flagged frame was recorded; the flags were
  spread across `pass`, `receive`, `get_up` and no action at all, i.e. no
  correlation with any clip.

Animation is exonerated by measurement, not by argument. The generated BoneMap
layer, 54/54 imports and the role-restricted mapping are untouched.

## 5. Success criterion

The criterion is zero occurrences of the intrusive slab from the real gameplay
camera. What can be stated from here:

- The object is identified and its dominant cause is removed, with a measured
  before/after at the pose that reproduced it.
- Across all 510 reachable camera poses, no object now composites to an opaque
  mass; the largest single-object coverage is 26% (an end stand at 6.1 m, seen
  from behind the goal line).

What cannot be stated from here: that a human will see zero occurrences. The
net still reads milky at very close range — its albedo alpha of 0.32 over 2–3
remaining layers is a design value, not a defect, and lowering it is available
if QA still sees it. **Human QA is the final authority and this is not claimed
fixed until the rendered build says so.**

## 6. Possession transfers — all fourteen fields, every transfer

`tests/diag_possession_trace.gd` logs every carrier change over a 90-second
match with the schema the brief lists: old and new carrier, teams, ball
position, both player positions, ball-to-new-carrier distance, body-to-body
distance, acquisition reason, challenge state, tackle state, contact evidence,
ball speed, controllability, and any grace or exemption open at that instant.

Two things had to be fixed in the tracer before its numbers meant anything, and
both are recorded because both looked like defects:

1. **Sampling lag.** Polling from `_physics_process` reads the world one step
   after possession is granted. A ball at 3 m/s has moved 0.05 m by then, which
   produced a run of acquisitions measuring 1.24–1.27 m against a 1.20 m gate —
   a consistent overshoot that looked exactly like a leak. The tracer now hooks
   `possession_changed`, so it records the geometry the gate actually saw.
2. **The loose-ball blind spot.** A carrier loses the ball, it is ownerless for
   a few frames, an opponent collects. Classifying that as an innocent "loose
   pickup" is how a real steal hides from a possession trace — 56 of the first
   run's 75 carrier changes were being excluded that way. Loose windows shorter
   than 0.5 s are now folded back into the turnover they belong to.

### Result, 90 s, corrected tracer

```
60 carrier changes, 10 of them opponent turnovers

acquisition reason        verdict
  CHALLENGE_PRESSURE   7    LEGITIMATE                8
  VIA_LOOSE_NO_CHALLENGE 3  NO_CONTACT_NO_CHALLENGE   2

mean ball->new-carrier distance at turnover:  0.84 m
worst:                                        1.18 m   (gate 1.20 m)
TURNOVERS FROM OUTSIDE THE ACQUISITION GATE:  0
```

The two `NO_CONTACT_NO_CHALLENGE` turnovers, read in full, are ordinary
football:

```
t=25.33  ball ran loose 0.20 s; collected at 1.16 m; bodies 1.47 m apart
t=31.07  ball ran loose 0.37 s; collected at 1.14 m; bodies 2.75 m apart
```

Both are inside the 1.20 m gate. That is an interception of a loose ball, not a
steal from range.

## 7 & 8. Did the slide widen the steal range? No

The slide is the only thing added to the acquisition path since QA last
accepted this geometry, so it was checked as constants and as behaviour.

**Reach, as constants:**

```
slide ball reach = SLIDE_EXTENT 1.25 + BALL_CONTACT 0.66 = 1.91 m
POKE_REACH                                                 2.00 m
CONTEST_WIN_REACH                                          1.70 m
POSSESSION_CONTACT_RADIUS                                  1.20 m
CHALLENGE_RANGE (awareness only)                           2.40 m
```

A slide cannot reach the ball from further than an ordinary poke, and the
contest-win collection reach stays inside the poke envelope. The visual tackle
buys no extra arm.

**Rate and effect, measured over the same 90 s:**

```
7 committed slides in 90 s  (4.7 per minute across all 22 players)
  CLEAN_TACKLE   5
  FOUL_CONTACT   1
  MISSED_TACKLE  1
turnovers attributable to a slide:  0 of 10
```

Slides are neither frequent nor a source of turnovers in live play. Nothing in
the v0.9.2.1 work changed collision shapes, player offsets, root orientation,
ControlArea placement or body-to-ball geometry — those are unchanged files, and
the geometry assertions above hold as constants.

Animation states, `challenge_started` and proximity still grant nothing:
`_on_challenge_animate` fires only on `kind == "slide"`, the CLEAN outcome is
decided by measured leg-segment-to-ball geometry before any animation, and
acquire-tight / retain-loose is untouched.

## 9. The six scenarios

`tests/v0_9_2_3_steal_test.gd`. Every scenario is staged **inside a real
match** — MatchManager spawns the 22 players, the AI and human input are
switched off, non-participants are parked and frozen at the touchline, and two
players plus the ball are placed. No threshold was relaxed to make anything
green.

```
[A] defender held 3.35 m from the ball for 3.0 s     -> no steal              PASS
[B] bodies touching at 0.90 m, ball 2.40 m away      -> no transfer           PASS
[C] defender reaches the ball (0.22 m)               -> wins it at 0.50 m     PASS
[D] slide committed at 7.60 m/s from 3.58 m,
    carrier cuts away                                -> MISSED_TACKLE, keeps  PASS
[E] loose ball at 1.00 m                             -> collected at 1.00 m   PASS
[F] 16 m/s ball crossing at 1.00 m                   -> no possession         PASS

19 passed, 0 failed
```

### The harness bugs this suite found in itself

The first three runs failed scenarios that were correct in the game, and the
causes are recorded rather than quietly patched, because each is the same
family of trap this milestone exists to avoid:

- **Assigning `velocity` does nothing.** `FootballPlayer._physics_process`
  rebuilds velocity from `move_input` every frame before `move_and_slide`, so a
  directly-assigned velocity is overwritten before it moves anybody. A defender
  that never moved was reported as "a loose ball at one metre cannot be
  collected". Same family as the v0.9.2.1 closing-speed trap.
- **A ball spawned inside a player's capsule is ejected at 240 m/s** and throws
  the player four metres sideways on the next frame. Measured frame by frame.
- **A hand-spawned player reported `is_on_floor()` while hanging at y = 1.52**
  and then teleported with zero velocity.

Every "impossible distance" the early runs printed came from the harness. Using
the game's own spawn path removed all three at once — which is also better
evidence, since what is now measured is the assembled game.

## 11. Found but deliberately not fixed

Per the brief, documented rather than acted on:

- **The goal nets still read milky at very close range.** Alpha 0.32 over 2–3
  layers. A design value, not a defect.
- **`CameraController` clamps nothing.** The rig follows
  `lerp(player, ball, 0.28)` in x and z with no bounds, so the camera can reach
  the goal mouth and put an end stand at 26% of the frame from 6.1 m. Nothing
  intersects, but the framing near the ends is loose. Out of scope here.
- **`V0_8_8PossessionValidityTest` check 12 is flaky.** "Midfielders keep
  adjusting when the ball is far" failed once at 7% moving and passed on both
  re-runs. Pre-existing non-determinism, unrelated to anything in this
  milestone.

## Honest summary

**Artifact:** found, named by object identity, root-caused to a
double-drawn translucent net, fixed with one flag, and the fix measured. Not
claimed closed until QA sees the build.

**Steals:** not reproducible. Zero turnovers from outside the acquisition gate
over 90 seconds; mean 0.84 m; six deterministic scenarios green with real
distances; the slide's reach and rate both cleared. If QA still sees it, the
one thing this pass could not exercise is a **human-driven carrier with live
touch input** — every measurement here is headless or AI-driven, and a human
holding the ball under touch control is the one configuration a headless run
cannot reproduce. That is the next place to look, and it needs a rendered
session with input, not another suite.
