## PRIORITY 0 — the visual artifact

### CONFIRMED: it is `Field/StandWestEnd` and `Field/StandEastEnd`

Named from the actual rendered pixels. `tests/diag_object_id.gd` renders an
**ID pass** alongside the real frame: every logical object gets a unique flat
unshaded colour, the sky is flattened to black and the UI is hidden, so
histogramming the image gives each object's **true screen coverage** with no
bounding-box approximation anywhere. When QA says "that thing", this names the
node.

Every earlier investigation reasoned from a proxy — whole-frame dark fraction,
bounding volumes, ray grids against AABBs — and each proxy found something real
while missing something real. That is why the artifact survived three passes.

Driving the camera through every pose the follow logic can reach:

```
camera |x|      StandWestEnd / StandEastEnd covers
   32              21.1%
   29               9.2%
   25               2.6%
   20               0.0%
```

The end stands are 3 × 5 × 40 m slabs with inner faces at x = ±36.5.
`CameraController` had **no bound of any kind** — it follows
`lerp(player, ball, 0.28)` and the ball can sit in a net at x = ±32.2 — so the
camera ended up **4.5 m from a forty-metre flat-shaded wall**. It fills a fifth
of the frame, is nearer than anything else on screen, has no texture and no
shading detail, and reads as corrupted geometry sweeping in from the side.

The saved frame pair (real + ids) for that pose is the direct evidence.

### The fix: contain the camera, not the stadium

Nothing is culled, no material changes, the stands render exactly as before.
The camera stops before pressing its nose against one. The axes are
deliberately asymmetric because the camera looks along −Z: the stand behind it
can never intrude; the one it faces can.

```gdscript
CAMERA_X_LIMIT        = 36.5 - 10.5   # end-stand inner face, minus clearance
CAMERA_Z_FORWARD_LIMIT = -16.0
```

### CONFIRMED: verified by re-running the same sweep with the shipped clamp

117 poses, rig driven to x −34..34 and z −24..24, then `_contain()` applied:

| object | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| end stand | **21.1 %** | **3.43 %** |
| goal net top | 12.9 % | 7.64 % |
| frames over 12 % | 14 | **0** |
| objects ever containing the camera | 0 | 0 |
| objects ever within 0.35 m | 0 | 0 |

The largest non-ground object is now `StandNorthTier1` at 10.8 % — the *far*
stand across the pitch, a backdrop band at the top of the frame, which is what
a stadium is supposed to look like.

### What this does NOT claim

Zero recurrence is the success criterion and only human QA can confirm it. What
is confirmed is that the object QA has been seeing is identified, that its
screen coverage is cut ~6×, and that no reachable camera pose now produces an
object over 12 % of the frame. **Not called fixed until the rendered build
says so.**

Also worth stating plainly: the previous two explanations were each *partly*
right and neither was complete. The ambient flag was a real defect and did
remove the black stands. The goal-net `cull_mode` was a real defect and did
halve a pale wash. Neither was this.

## PRIORITY 1 — Football AI 2.0

### What was built

**`FootballPerception`** — one snapshot per team per frame, built once and read
by the plan, by every duty target and by the carrier. It answers the football
questions that were previously answered independently at several call sites
with different radii and different weights:

- pressure on a player, and pressure at a hypothetical point
- open space at a point (opponents *and* touchline pinning)
- teammates already near a point, so runs are not duplicated
- passing-lane quality, measured perpendicular to the lane and ignoring
  opponents behind the passer
- danger at a point, weighted for central vs wide
- goal-side test, progression along the forward axis
- **anticipation**: a bounded 1.1 s damped projection of the ball, the point
  where it becomes controllable, and an interception point that solves "when
  can I get there" against "where will it be then"

Cost is O(players × opponents) **once per team per frame**, not per player per
frame.

**Two new duties**, both of which are things a player does *instead* of going
near the ball:

- `INTERCEPT` — stand in the pass, not next to the ball
- `REST_DEFENCE` — the deepest players stay deep on purpose

### CONFIRMED: two wrong guesses, and what the measurement actually said

The first version allocated `INTERCEPT` on **0.0 %** of sampled frames. Guess
one (the danger multiplier was zeroing midfield lanes) and guess two (the
attack-intent threshold) were both plausible and both wrong. So
`tests/diag_intercept_gate.gd` counted every gate over 5400 team-frames:

```
we do not have the ball        87.9%
attack intent allows it        45.0%
an opponent is CARRYING         5.8%   <- the collapse
a lane clears the bar           1.3%
```

**The ball in this game is loose far more often than it is held.** A duty that
only exists during settled build-up play exists 0.1 % of the time. This is a
real finding about the game's character and it is also, probably, the deeper
reason midfield has always looked passive — there is rarely a build-up phase to
participate in.

The correction: a ball already travelling toward an opponent is the same
football problem. The lane is the carrier's best pass when there is a carrier,
and the ball's own projected path when there is not. `CONTEST` still sends
exactly one player *at* the ball; `INTERCEPT` is the second player getting in
*front* of it — which is a defensive unit rather than a swarm.

After the correction: **INTERCEPT 2.6 %, REST_DEFENCE 6.2 %** of duty
allocations.

### CONFIRMED: deterministic proof — `tests/v1_0_ai_test.gd`, 22/22

```
pressure at 12 m        0.00        pressure at 1.2 m      0.67
empty lane              1.00        lane with a body in it 0.04
opponent BEHIND passer  1.00  (cannot intercept what has gone past)
ball read leads by      8.82 m      and is bounded
interceptor placed      0.00 m off the carrier->receiver line
                        6.83 m from the ball
rest defence held       2 players, all defenders, at the cap
```

The interceptor being 6.83 m from the ball while sitting exactly on the passing
line is the single number that says this is not ball-chasing.

### LIKELY / UNKNOWN: whether it looks better

Full-match A/B telemetry (`tests/diag_ai_telemetry.gd`, paired runs on one
build, 75 s each):

| measure | unit defending OFF | ON |
|---|---|---|
| defenders within 8 m of the ball (mean) | 0.73 | 0.77 |
| midfielders on a job other than holding shape | 75.0 % | 72.2 % |
| turnovers per minute | 21.6 | 22.4 |

Across the four A/B pairs run during development the OFF baseline alone moved
between 0.73 and 1.04 on clustering and 16.0 and 21.6 on turnovers. **These
aggregate deltas are inside run-to-run noise and nothing is claimed from
them.** What *is* confirmed is that both duties are allocated at a meaningful
rate and place players correctly, which is a structural change; whether it
reads as better football is for rendered QA.

One number worth flagging honestly: defensive clustering was already low
(mean ≈ 1 defender within 8 m of the ball, worst case 6). The "ten ball
chasers" impression is therefore **not** what the counts show, and the AI 2.0
duties are not attacking the thing QA is describing. That gap needs rendered
observation to close, not more headless tuning.

## HANDOFF — what remains, and where to start

Nothing below was started. Ordered by the brief's own priority.

### Priority 1, remaining

1. **Carrier decision model.** `AIController._decide_possession_action` already
   compares shoot/pass/dribble on a common "ratio past its own bar" basis
   (v0.9.1.1) — that is the right shape and should be extended, not replaced.
   What is missing is classifying `PassEvaluator` options into
   through / progressive / safe / recycle and adding `HOLD` and `TURN` as
   first-class actions. `FootballPerception` now supplies the inputs
   (`progression`, `lane_quality`, `pressure_at`, `space_at`).
2. **Off-ball attacking variety.** `TeamPlan` already allocates
   `SUPPORT_SHORT / SUPPORT_WIDE / RUN_BEHIND / PUSH_UP`; the gap is that their
   targets are geometric. `perception.teammates_near()` exists specifically so
   a run into an occupied zone can be rejected — it is not wired in yet.
3. **Intent persistence.** `TeamPlan.DUTY_RETENTION_BONUS` gives duty-level
   stickiness. A per-player *movement* intent with a minimum dwell does not
   exist.
4. **Scenarios A–J.** `tests/v1_0_ai_test.gd` covers I (protect the central
   lane) and part of F (cover vs runner). A–E, G, H, J remain. Use its
   `_match()` harness — staging inside a real match is load-bearing; a
   hand-built scene does not reproduce the game's spawn.

### Priority 2 — match state and rules

Not started. `MatchManager` currently owns kickoff, goals and the clock
directly. A `MatchState` node should own the state machine and emit events, and
`MatchManager` should become a consumer. Fouls already have a foundation:
`SlideTackle` resolves `FOUL_CONTACT` from measured geometry and
`FootballPlayer.fouled` is emitted with position and duration — nothing
consumes it. That signal is the correct place to start.

### Priority 3 — UI and presentation

Not started. `scripts/HUD.gd` (144 lines) and `scenes/UI/HUD.tscn` are the
prototype to replace; touch controls live there and must be preserved.

1. **Does the intrusive geometry still appear?** This is the release blocker
   and the only thing that can close it is playing the build. The end stands

2. **Does the AI look different?** The two new duties are structural. The

3. **Anything the camera containment broke.** The camera now stops at
   |x| ≤ 26. Play in the goal mouth is still framed, but this changes the feel
