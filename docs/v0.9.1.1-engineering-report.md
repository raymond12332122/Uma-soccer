# v0.9.1.1 — AI Brain + Goalkeeper Recovery

Engineering report. Every number was measured on this build; the diagnostics
are checked in under `tests/diag_*.gd` and can be re-run. Hypotheses that were
falsified are recorded rather than deleted, because several of them were mine
and the record is the point.

---

## 1. GOALKEEPER REGRESSION — root cause found, and it was mine

Human QA: *"goalkeepers are noticeably dumber and sometimes fail to attempt a
block/save when the ball is dangerously close."*

Literally true. **The ball passed through every player on the pitch.**

```
player layer 2 mask 3 | ball layer 4 mask 1
player blocked by ball? false   ball blocked by player? false
>>> THE BALL PASSED THROUGH THE PLAYER
```

Introduced in v0.9.1 in two steps. Fixing "players phase through each other"
set the player mask to 3, removing the ball layer. Shooting then broke (the
ball rebounded off the shooter's own capsule), and I "decoupled" by removing
players from the *ball's* mask as well. Godot masks are directional, so with
neither body listing the other, they stopped interacting entirely. There was
nothing for any keeper — or defender — to block with.

**My own v0.9.1 suite passed *because* of this bug.** It asserted "a ball
struck at 25 m/s does not launch the player it hits (moved 0.00m)". A player
cannot be moved by a ball that never touches them. The assertion was right and
was satisfied by a catastrophic failure; no check in that suite could have
caught it, because the check encoded the same mistake. Human QA found it.

### A second, deeper cause

Restoring the mask was not sufficient. Measured with a ball fired at a
stationary keeper:

```
f29  ball z=1.05  vz=-9.58  poss=false
f30  ball z=0.94  vz=-6.10  poss=TRUE     <- possession granted at 9.58 m/s
```

Possession was granted at 1.05m while the ball was still doing **9.58 m/s**,
and close control then damped it. No save was ever *required*: a struck ball
was handed over on contact with the possession radius. Distance decides
whether you can reach a ball; it now also has to be controllable when you get
there — acquisition is refused above `CONTROLLED_BALL_SPEED` (6.0 m/s
relative). Retention is untouched, so dribbling is unaffected by construction.

The carrier and the kicker still need to pass through the ball — close control
would otherwise fight its own capsule, and a shot leaves from inside the
shooter's. Those are now targeted, self-expiring exceptions
(`BallController.pass_through_for`) rather than a blanket rule.

```
before: possession at 9.58 m/s -> ball travelled through the body
after:  ball stopped by the body 0.62m in front, collected at 0.6 m/s
```

## 2. GOALKEEPER THREAT MODEL

The keeper previously had **no save behaviour of any kind**:
`update_goalkeeper` was four constants and one positional target. It worked
only because anyone standing near a ball absorbed it.

It now picks an intention from the actual danger — ball distance and velocity,
whether the trajectory is on target, time to goal, whether an attacker owns
it, and its own distance to ball and goal:

`POSITION` · `CLOSE_ANGLE` · `ATTACK_LOOSE_BALL` · `BLOCK` · `SAVE` · `RECOVER`

Positioning remains the default; a genuine threat overrides it.

### Scenario results (13/13)

| scenario | result |
|---|---|
| ball fired at a stationary keeper | stopped 4.66m on the near side, does not pass through |
| close shot, 14 m/s from 10m | recognised as SAVE/BLOCK, **reaction 0.17s**, closest approach **0.60m** |
| uncontested loose ball 4m out | claims it, travels 4.13m, reaches 0.81m |
| harmless ball 25m away moving off | stays passive, ends 3.53m from goal |
| ball walked around the attacking half | furthest excursion 3.55m — never abandons the goal |

The close shot still went in. Per the brief, a *believable attempt* is the
requirement, not a save every time.

**One fix inside the fix.** The first claim rule keyed danger off
keeper-to-ball distance, so after claiming and clearing, the keeper chased its
own clearance **13.26m** upfield — and the "did it go for the ball" assertion
passed for entirely the wrong reason. Danger is now measured from the GOAL: a
keeper claims balls in and around their own box.

## 3. THE CARRIER DECISION — a cascade, not a comparison

Human QA: *"attacker has a clear opportunity toward goal; instead of shooting,
AI sometimes chooses a pointless backward pass."*

The cause was structural:

```
if in range and shoot_score >= shoot_threshold  -> SHOOT
elif pass option and option.score >= pass_threshold -> PASS
else -> dribble
```

Shoot and pass were scored on **completely separate scales and never
compared**. A shot at 0.55 against its 0.56 bar fell through and lost to a
backward pass at 0.87 against its 0.86 — the shot was a chance, the pass was
worthless, and nothing in the code could express that. Dribble was whatever
was left when both failed.

Each action now reports how far it clears **its own calibrated bar** — a
ratio, so every existing threshold and all prior tuning is preserved exactly —
and the best ratio wins. The full decision is recorded (`decision_made`,
`last_decision`): every candidate, its utility, its bar, and why the losers
lost, as the brief requires.

### Scoring opportunity (brief section 6)

A single 0..1 description of the chance — range, signed angle, lane, composure
— deliberately separate from `shoot_score`, which is a threshold test. A high
opportunity discounts a **backward** pass in proportion to how good the chance
is and how far backwards the ball would go. A discount, never a veto.

## 4. SCENARIO BATTERY (brief section 8)

| | expected | chose | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A obvious shot | SHOOT | **SHOOT** | clears bar 1.62x, opportunity 0.58 |
| B blocked lane | PASS/reposition | **PASS** | 1.32x |
| C terrible angle | PASS can beat SHOOT | **DRIBBLE** | shot scores 0.00 — does not shoot from a hopeless angle |
| D 1v1 | shoot or progress | **SHOOT** | 1.38x |
| E pressure x2 | PASS | **PASS** | pressure 0.58 |
| F open field | DRIBBLE/progress | **DRIBBLE** | no absurd-range shot |
| G useful backward pass | backward PASS | **PASS** | progress **-0.58** — backward passes survive |

Suite: `V0_9_1_1DecisionTest`, **17/17 green**, including contextual-opportunity
checks (a blocked lane scores 0.19 vs 0.58 clear; a wide angle 0.06 vs 0.58),
no-shot-spam checks, and personality bounds.

### Two overcorrections of mine, both caught by measurement

1. **Shot avoidance.** My first dribble utility was unbounded; D dribbled past
   a viable 1v1 shot (dribble 1.57 vs shot 1.38). Carrying is now valued by
   what it *adds* — scaled by `(1 - opportunity)` — so it cannot win in front
   of goal.
2. **Never releasing.** The dribble ratio was on a different scale from the
   others (up to 2.0 against ~1.0-1.6), so with nobody near, a carrier held
   the ball for the full ten seconds of v0_8_3's test — the "carriers keep
   possession too long" complaint, reintroduced by me while fixing the
   opposite. Rescaled onto the same axis, and `must_release` (pressure, held
   too long, challenge landing) now discounts carrying to 35%, which it had
   silently stopped doing at all.

A third: my pressure fallback initially allowed a below-bar pass on the first
frame of possession — pass-spam. It now requires a touch first.

## 5. THE MOST IMPORTANT MEASUREMENT — and it reframes the complaint

`diag_decision_mix`, 90s of live 11v11, tapping every player's decision:

```
969 decisions over 90s
  SHOOT        1        PASS  89        DRIBBLE  879
scoring opportunity -- median 0.000, p90 0.022, MAX 0.289
decisions taken with a good chance on (>= 0.45): 0
backward passes 31, of which 0 were played with a good chance on
```

(DRIBBLE is a per-FRAME record — the decision runs every frame a carrier holds
the ball — so 879 is not an action count. The discrete actions are 89 passes
and 1 shot.)

Two findings:

**My section-6 protection was inert.** `HIGH_OPPORTUNITY` was 0.45, chosen
from how the constructed scenarios scored. The live match never exceeds
**0.289**, so the backward-pass penalty could never fire in real play — it
only ever worked in scenarios I had built to be good chances. Recalibrated to
**0.20** against the measured distribution: far above the real p90 of 0.022,
and below scenarios B (0.189) and G (0.195), which must still pass backwards.
All 17 checks and all 7 scenarios unchanged after the change.

**Zero backward passes were played with a good chance on.** The specific QA
behaviour did not occur in 90 seconds of play.

## 6. UNRESOLVED — reported, not hidden

### The AI rarely creates chances at all

Peak scoring opportunity over a full match is **0.289**; 1 shot per 45s
playtest. That is chance *creation*, not action *selection* — when a chance
exists the AI takes it (scenario A, D). It is **pre-existing, not caused by
this milestone**: the v0.9.1 playtest also recorded 1 shot (43 passes), as does
v0.9.1.1 (34 passes).

Fixing it means off-ball movement and attacking structure, which sections 11
and 18 place out of scope. It is the single biggest remaining obstacle to the
game looking like football, and it is the thing I would do next.

### Kickoff is more contested

```
                    first carrier      carrier frames, first 5s
v0.9.1.1            frame 22 (0.37s)         18 of 300 (6%)
v0.9.0 baseline     frame 22 (0.37s)         35 of 300 (12%)
```

Possession is not delayed — first carrier at frame 22 in both — but it is
retained about half as often during the opening scramble, when 22 players
converge on one ball that now deflects off bodies and must settle before
anyone can claim it. `V0_8_3`'s two sample-size guards need >30 and measure 18,
identically on all four runs.

Over a full match the same build is **better** than baseline: 44% of frames
have a carrier against 30%, with mean possession spells of 0.68s against 0.29s.
No threshold was changed, and a correct physics fix was not reverted to
satisfy a five-second window.

### Duty churn sits close to its bar

Six runs against a <0.30 bar: 0.185, 0.277, 0.226, 0.140, 0.227, 0.292 --
all pass, mean 0.225. One full-battery run measured 0.317, so it does
occasionally cross. Reported as "passes, with an outlier" rather than
"fixed", because the margin is thin.

### Carried over from v0.9.1

- **Off-ball run convergence** — reduced by roughly half in v0.9.1, not
  eliminated. Untouched here.
- **`V0_8_6` spawn overlap** — harness artifact: two `CharacterBody3D`s created
  in one frame are resolved against each other before either takes a physics
  step.
- **Ball-induced player displacement rose to 0.727m** (from 0.321m) in the
  rendered playtest — a direct consequence of restoring ball/player collision.
  Under the 0.8m bar, but worth watching.

### Passing-lane share: improved, NOT fixed

v0.9.1 shipped this as an open regression. It is much better and still not
green, and the distinction matters -- one battery run showed GREEN and I very
nearly reported it as fixed on that single sample. Six runs, bar is >50%:

```
v0.9.0 baseline    4 of 4 pass
v0.9.1 as shipped  1 of 5 pass -- 43, 36, 37, 26  (mean ~35%)
v0.9.1.1           3 of 6 pass -- 48, 56, 44, 48, 52, 52  (mean 50.0%)
```

Better carrier decisions raised it by roughly fifteen points without the lane
code being touched, and it now sits exactly ON its bar rather than well below
it. It is still a partially-open regression carried from v0.9.1, not a fix.

## 7. RENDERED PLAYTEST

**AI vs AI, 45s.** Ball/carrier separation 0.77m over 780 carrier frames, 91
touches (7.0/s). 34 passes, 1 shot. **0 passes aimed at an opponent.**
Teammates bunched on the ball 0% of carrier frames. Forwards ahead of the ball
66%. 0 kicks by a non-carrier; kicks struck from 0.91m average, 1.40m furthest
against a 2.4m challenge range. Closest any two players came: 0.77m, with 0
overlapping pairs.

**Human vs AI, 45s**, against the same measures in v0.9.1:

| | v0.9.1 | v0.9.1.1 |
|---|---|---|
| human passes reaching the intended mate | 7/12 (58%) | **9/12 (75%)** |
| human pass angular error, mean | 25.7 deg | **17.4 deg** |
| human pass angular error, worst | 58.3 deg | **37.0 deg** |
| dispossessed in 45s | 17 | 18 |
| under active challenge | 40% | 57% |
| passes aimed at an opponent | 0 | 0 |

Qualitatively: possession changes hands through contested challenges; the
keeper now reacts to shots and comes for loose balls in its box; passing is
noticeably more purposeful. The weakest thing on screen remains attacking
structure — the ball is worked around midfield without often arriving in a
position worth shooting from, which is the chance-creation limitation above.

## Honest summary

Closed: the goalkeeper regression (ball/player collision, possession
absorption, and a real threat model), the shoot-vs-pass comparison, contextual
action selection across seven scenarios, personality bounded within football
logic, and — as a side effect — v0.9.1's open passing-lane regression.

Open: the AI rarely creates good chances (pre-existing, out of scope here);
kickoff possession is more contested than baseline; off-ball run convergence
and the `V0_8_6` spawn artifact carry over unchanged.

Four hypotheses of mine were falsified by measurement during this milestone:
that restoring the mask alone would fix the keeper, that possession had become
too fragmented (it improved), that `V0_8_3`'s guards were failing on
distribution lumpiness (they are deterministic and kickoff-specific), and that
"released after -0.02s" meant an instant release (it means never). Each was
corrected by instrumenting the exact frame rather than reasoning from the code.
