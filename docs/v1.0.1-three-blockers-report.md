## BLOCKER 1 — the visual artifact

### CONFIRMED: identified, fixed, and still holding

Named from actual rendered pixels by an object-ID pass
(`tests/diag_object_id.gd`): every object gets a unique flat unshaded colour,
the sky is flattened and the UI hidden, so histogramming the frame gives each
object's **true screen coverage**. It is `Field/StandWestEnd` and
`Field/StandEastEnd` — 3 × 5 × 40 m slabs whose inner faces sit at x = ±36.5,
with an unclamped camera that followed the ball to x = ±32 and ended up 4.5 m
from a forty-metre flat-shaded wall.

Fixed by containing the camera inside the bowl (nothing culled, no material
changed). Re-verified this session on the current build:

| object | before the fix | now |
|---|---|---|
| `StandEastEnd` | **21.1 %** | **4.94 %** |
| `StandWestEnd` | 21.1 % | 3.26 % |
| `GoalLeftNetTop` | 12.9 % | 8.97 % |
| frames over 12 % | 14 | 1 |

The one frame over threshold is `StandNorthTier1` at 12.9 % — the *far* stand
across the pitch, which renders as a horizon band across the top of the frame
with the camera sitting at exactly both clamp limits (26.0, −16.0). Inspected
directly: it reads as a stadium backdrop, not an intrusion.

`V1_0AiTest` also proves the shipped follow logic applies the clamp: the
controlled player runs to |x| = 31.60 and the camera stops at 26.00.

### NOT CLOSED

**This blocker is not closed until human QA confirms the intrusive geometry is
gone.** What is confirmed is the object's identity, a ~5× reduction in its
screen coverage, and that no reachable camera pose now produces a wall.

## BLOCKER 3 — fall / collision / recovery synchronisation

### CONFIRMED: it was real, and it is fixed

The defect was in this project's own v0.9.2.1 code, and its comment said so out
loud — *"the recovery clip plays exactly once, on the frame control returns"*.
Movement unlocked when the DOWN timers expired and `get_up` was fired on that
same frame, so the recovery animation played from frame zero while the player
was already free to sprint.

Measured before (`tests/diag_recovery_sync.gd`), one knockdown, human holding
the stick:

```
control returned at frame        77    STUMBLE_TIME 1.30 s x 60
the get-up clip finished at      176   its 1.65 s tail, 99 frames later
desync                           99 frames
distance sprinted while rising   9.68 m
```

Ten metres of running while visibly picking yourself up off the floor.

Getting up is now an explicit second phase (`is_rising`) that owns the body
until it is genuinely finished, and "finished" is a property of the
**animation** rather than of a timer running beside it —
`AnimationController.is_action_playing()` answers for the real-clip path and
the procedural path alike, so half-speed playback extends the hold without
anyone tracking a rate.

Bounded three ways, because no reaction may ever freeze a player: a safety
ceiling for a clip that never reports finishing, a fallback hold for a rig with
no clips, and a floor before "not playing" is believed (the tree advances on
idle frames while this runs on physics frames).

Measured after — `tests/v1_0_recovery_test.gd`, **23/23**:

| condition | control returns | moved while locked |
|---|---|---|
| human input | 177 | **0.000 m** |
| no input | 177 | 0.000 m |
| half-speed playback | **276** | 0.000 m |
| no animation layer | 110 (fallback) | 0.000 m |
| slide recovery | 130 | 0.000 m |
| knocked down again mid-rise | cancels the rise, still recovers | — |
| match reset | clears both phases outright | — |

Zero desync and zero planar movement while grounded in every condition, and the
half-speed case extends the hold rather than desynchronising — which is the
property that separates an authoritative recovery from a tuned timer.

`BallContest` and `SlideTackle` now gate on `is_recovering()` rather than on
`stumble_time` alone, so a player still getting up neither accrues challenge
progress nor launches a slide. The clean / foul / missed / avoided slide
outcomes are intact (`V0_9_2_1QATest` 37/37).

## BLOCKER 2 — goalkeeper intelligence

### CONFIRMED: the threat model was never the problem

`V0_9_1_1GoalkeeperTest` was 13/13 while QA still called this a blocker, so the
suite was measuring the wrong thing. Live measurement
(`tests/diag_keeper_live.gd`, 150 s matches):

```
the keeper was in POSITION on          98-99% of frames
at EVERY goal conceded they were already in SAVE
...0.83 m to 3.10 m from the ball, half a second before it went in
BLOCK fired on 0.0-0.1% of frames, CLOSE_ANGLE on 0.0%
save_left / save_right / catch clips shipped in v0.9.2
AIController called play_action                    ZERO times
```

The keeper reads the shot and goes to the right place. **Nothing made their
body stop the ball.** At 0.83 m from a ball doing 13.7 m/s, with contact
needing 0.56 m, walking toward an interception point is not a save. Exactly the
same inversion as the visual artifact: the pieces existed and nothing joined
them.

### The fix

`GoalkeeperSave` — a committed dive resolved by measured geometry, on the
proven `SlideTackle` pattern: a direction locked in at commit and never
re-aimed, a reach that is the **segment** swept by an outstretched dive rather
than a bubble, and an outcome (CAUGHT / PARRIED / MISSED) decided before any
clip is chosen. The dive costs a recovery — the keeper lands on the floor and
gets up through the phase added for blocker 3 — so diving at nothing is a real
mistake.

Bounded to a person: 1.30 m extent + 0.66 m contact = **1.96 m of reach**, a
0.75–4.20 m commit window, a cooldown, and a lateral-need condition.

### Two corrections the tests forced

1. `can_commit` measured the gap **to** the ball, which is large for a ball
   coming straight at the keeper — who should stand up. Measured, that had a
   keeper committing at 0.89 m and **missing a ball they were already in front
   of**. What decides a dive is the *lateral* offset, so `STAND_REACH` (0.95 m)
   now gates it from below and the dive reach from above.
2. `begin_dive` re-aimed a dive already in flight, making it a homing dive.
   Guarded, as `begin_slide` already was.

Deterministic — `tests/v1_0_keeper_test.gd`, **14/14**: 0.2 m lateral → stand
up; 1.4 m → dive; 3.4 m → do not throw yourself at it; a dive across the path
CAUGHT; commitment locked; the dive always ends and always recovers.

Live after: 1 dive committed, 1 CAUGHT, 0 missed.

### HONEST LIMIT — this blocker is the least finished of the three

The goals still conceded come from situations the dive does not cover: a
six-yard scramble (ball at 0.0 m/s, keeper in `ATTACK_LOOSE_BALL`) and a
close-angle chance. Those are **different problems**, not this one.
`CLOSE_ANGLE` fires on ~0.1 % of frames and `BLOCK` on ~0.0 %, so two of the six
intents are effectively dead code. Goals per minute remains high (0.4–1.6
across runs) and run-to-run variance in this project is large enough to swallow
the effect either way — **no scoreline claim is made.** If QA still finds the
keeper weak, the next thing to attack is the close-range scramble, not the
dive.

### Harness errors recorded rather than quietly fixed

Four this session, all of the same family — the measurement being wrong rather
than the game:

- the keeper AI must be driven for the keeper to move at all; a first run read
  a 6.36 m closest approach as "does not commit"
- a 10 m/s shot from 9 m arrives at **4.1 m/s** and is correctly reclassified
  `SAVE → ATTACK_LOOSE_BALL`; the test misunderstood the ball physics
- **GDScript lambdas capture locals by value**, so a signal handler assigning to
  a captured int reported "no outcome" while the dive was in fact resolving
- the keeper's own lateral positioning covers ±3.5 m, so a constructed
  "reachable" shot kept landing in the band positioning already handles; the
  commit rule is now tested directly rather than through 180 frames of
  emergent movement

## What requires human QA

1. **Is the intrusive geometry gone?** The release blocker. Confirmed cause,
   ~5× coverage reduction, no reachable pose producing a wall — but only
   playing the build can close it.
2. **Does a knocked-over player now look right?** They stay put for about three
   seconds and get up before moving. That is correct but it is *longer* than
   before, and it may read as sluggish in the hand.
3. **Does the keeper look better?** Dives are rare by design (they only fire
   when a dive is genuinely needed). The remaining weakness is close-range
   scrambles, which this pass did not address.
