# v0.9.2 — 54-Animation Integration Milestone

Characters are now animated by the Soccer Animation Pack. Gameplay was not
changed to accommodate it: no decision, threshold, radius or impulse from
v0.9.1.1 moved, and the animation layer reads the simulation through the event
surface v0.9.1 already emits without ever writing back to it.

This report gives the honest counts asked for in section 32 first, then how
each part was built and what it cost, then what is measurably still wrong.

---

## Counts (section 32)

| Category | Count | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **IMPORTED** | 54 / 54 | every FBX imports cleanly through Godot 4.3's native `ufbx` |
| **COMPATIBLE** | 54 / 54 | after retargeting, every clip's 22 bone tracks land on a real bone on all 11 character rigs — 0 misses, verified per character |
| **MAPPED** | 38 / 54 | wired to a gameplay meaning in `AnimationSet`. The other 16 are listed there with the reason each was left out |
| **ACTIVE** | 18 | clips a running match can actually reach today: 8 locomotion + 2 idles + 8 behind the 9 action intents that gameplay fires |
| **VALIDATED** | 18 | every ACTIVE clip is covered by an assertion in `V0_9_2AnimationTest` that fails if it stops resolving |

**ACTIVE is not "everything works".** 20 mapped clips — headers, volleys,
throw-ins, getting up, and 11 of the 15 goalkeeper clips — are built, cropped
and in the shared library, but nothing in the game calls for them, because no
gameplay state means "throw-in" or "knocked down" yet. They are one line of
wiring away, and that line is not this milestone's to write.

And of the 18 ACTIVE, the 25-second rendered match exercised them unevenly.
Per-player samples of the locomotion blend:

```
forward   284 (52%)      left   9 (2%)
idle      251 (46%)      right  5 (1%)
back        1 (0%)
```

The backpedal and strafe clips are wired, blended and correct, and the match
essentially never asks for them, because players turn to face where they are
going. Reporting them as "integrated" is true; implying you will see them is
not.

## 1. The retarget layer, and a failure that reported nothing

The pack and the models share no bone names at all:

```
pack     65 bones,  root 'mixamorig_Hips',  Mixamo naming
models   399-440 bones, root '_rootJoint',  Hip_04, Waist_0102, Sp_Hi_MSkirt0_B_00_05
```

and every character has a different bone count, so the numeric suffix shifts
from model to model (`Hip_04` on Gold Ship, `Hip_05` on Tokai Teio).
Hand-authoring twelve BoneMaps would be unmaintainable and would break on the
next re-export, so they are **generated** (`tools/generate_bone_maps.gd`): each
humanoid slot has a regex, matched against the real skeleton. The regexes are
anchored and digit-suffixed on purpose — a bare prefix match grabs the decoys
these rigs are full of (`Head_attach_0178`, `Ankle_offset_L_070`).

Result: **22/22 slots on all 11 characters and on the pack, zero ambiguous
matches.**

Import-time renaming was then switched on so `Hip_04` and `mixamorig_Hips` both
become `Hips`, after which a clip authored on one rig plays on the other at no
per-frame cost — which is the whole reason this is done at import and not at
runtime, with 22 animated players on screen.

### The failure worth recording

The first attempt wrote the `retarget/*` options as top-level scene-import
options. Godot **silently dropped every one of them** on the next reimport and
reported nothing: no error, no warning, an `.import` file with no retarget
block, and characters with 0/19 humanoid slots renamed.

They are not top-level options. They are *internal* options belonging to a
specific `Skeleton3D`, read out of the `.import` file's `_subresources`:

```
_subresources={ "nodes": { "PATH:<path to the skeleton>": { "retarget/bone_map": ... } } }
```

The key is the node's import id, defaulting to `"PATH:"` plus its path from the
scene root — a different five-level path on every character
(`Sketchfab_model/<hash>_fbx/RootNode/Object_3/Skeleton3D`), so
`tools/apply_retarget_imports.gd` reads it off the real imported scene rather
than assuming it.

**What caught it:** `tests/diag_retarget_verify.gd`, written *before* building
anything on top, which asks the narrow checkable question — do the two rigs now
use the same bone names, and do the clip's tracks address bones that exist? It
answered 0/19 and 52 misses. Had I gone straight to the state machine, the
symptom would have been characters standing still for no visible reason.

## 2. Root motion: removed, for two independent reasons (section 3)

Every clip carries a hips position track, and for the locomotion clips that
track is the gait's whole ground travel — `jog forward` moves the hips 2.15 m
across its 0.82 s cycle. Played as authored, the model walks 2.15 m away from
the CharacterBody carrying it and snaps back on the loop.

So `AnimationLibraryCache` flattens the horizontal part of the hips track at
build time, keeping the vertical bob. The design argument is section 3's: the
CharacterBody is authoritative and the animation never moves the player.

Measurement then supplied a second, independent reason. Retargeting normalises
position tracks against the source skeleton's proportions and rescales them
onto the target, and that rescaling does not survive these rigs' skins, whose
bind poses carry a 0.01 scale: **the applied hips translation comes out around
24 m per cycle on a 1.6 m character** — twenty times its own body height. The
vertical channel is fine (it sits at the rest height and varies by a few
centimetres, which is why the bob is kept and looks right). On this rig the
horizontal hips track is not merely unwanted, it is wrong.

`V0_9_2AnimationTest` asserts no clip in the shipped library translates across
the ground at all (worst case: 0.000000).

## 3. Locomotion (sections 5–7)

An `AnimationTree` per player over one shared clip library:

```
Idle ------\
            Blend2 (Loco) ---\
Move -> TimeScale -/          OneShot (Shot) --> output
Action ----------------------/
```

`Move` is an eight-point directional blend space driven by the CharacterBody's
**actual velocity**, rotated into the frame the player faces. A player running
north while facing east strafes, and that falls out of the geometry with no
special case.

### Which clip goes where was measured, not read off the filenames

Mixamo ships the two forward diagonals under identical names (`jog forward
diagonal` and `... (2)`), so left and right can only be told apart by measuring
which way the hips go. Angles relative to `jog forward`:

| clip | angle | travel | duration | natural speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| jog forward | 0.0° | 2.15 m | 0.82 s | 2.62 m/s |
| jog forward diagonal | +45.0° | 2.31 m | 0.78 s | 2.95 m/s |
| jog forward diagonal (2) | −45.4° | 2.27 m | 0.83 s | 2.73 m/s |
| jog strafe right | +89.8° | 1.76 m | 0.72 s | 2.45 m/s |
| jog strafe left | −90.0° | 2.11 m | 0.70 s | 3.01 m/s |
| jog backward diagonal | +134.5° | 1.68 m | 0.72 s | 2.35 m/s |
| jog backward diagonal (2) | −135.5° | 2.13 m | 0.82 s | 2.61 m/s |
| jog backward | ±180° | 1.90 m | 0.78 s | 2.43 m/s |

A clean eight-way rosette, and the filenames' own left/right labelling matched
the measurement.

### Playback rate, and the foot slide that is left

Playback rate is ground speed divided by how far the gait carries *that*
character. That figure is per-rig, because the roster differs by 19% here —
they are normalised to the same height but leg length as a fraction of height
is not the same:

```
gold_ship 3.49   oguri_cap 3.42   symboli_rudolf 3.38   air_groove 3.38
silence_suzuka 3.30   mejiro_mcqueen 3.26   tm_opera_o 3.20
grass_wonder 3.12   tokai_teio 3.08   agnes_digital 2.93   tamamo_cross 2.87
```

(mean 3.22 m/s — note how far that is from the pack's own 2.64 m/s: driving the
rate off the pack figure would have every player's feet running 22% slow.)

Rate is clamped to 0.55–1.90. Below the floor the legs crawl while the body
slides; above the ceiling a jog clip stops reading as a person running. The
consequence, stated plainly:

| gameplay speed | wanted rate | used | feet cycle for | slide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| walk 2.0 m/s | 0.62 | 0.62 | 2.00 m/s | none |
| base run 5.0 m/s | 1.55 | 1.55 | 5.00 m/s | none |
| sprint 8.5 m/s | 2.64 | **1.90** | 6.12 m/s | **+2.38 m/s (28%)** |

**Sprinting slides.** The pack contains one gait and no sprint, and a
rate-scaled jog is not a real sprint. In the rendered match only **3% of
samples** ran into the ceiling (median rate 0.61, p90 1.66), so this is a
visible flaw at top speed rather than a constant one — but it is a flaw, and no
amount of tuning removes it. A sprint clip would.

## 4. Strike timing, and why the two clips called "kick" are unused

Gameplay decides to pass and launches the ball on the same frame —
FootballPlayer's own event documentation says `action_started` "fires on the
same frame as the contact today (the simulation has no wind-up)". There is no
telegraph to hide a wind-up behind, and delaying the ball to wait for the
animation would make the animation authoritative over gameplay, which the brief
forbids.

So a strike **starts at its measured contact frame**: the boot is at peak speed
on the frame the ball leaves, and what plays is the follow-through. Which makes
the follow-through the only part that is ever seen — and that decides the clip
choice. Measured (duration − contact) for every strike-shaped clip:

```
soccer penalty kick   0.76s      scissor kick          2.00s
header soccerball     1.46s      header soccerball (2) 4.00s
kneeing soccerball(2) 0.51s      stall soccerball (4)  0.42s
kick soccerball       0.06s      kick soccerball (2)   0.09s
kick up soccerball    0.10s      kneeing soccerball    0.14s
```

The bottom rows are why the clips actually named `kick soccerball` are **not**
the pass and shot animations: three to six frames remain after the boot meets
the ball, which is not a kick anybody can see.

**Falsified hypothesis, recorded rather than quietly dropped.** I suspected the
FBX importer's `animation/trimming` default had eaten the follow-through, and
reimported all 54 with it off. Durations came back byte-identical: the clips
genuinely end at contact. The setting is left off because importing what the
file contains is the more honest default, not because it fixed anything.

### Measured lag (section 27)

**The ball has travelled 0.43 m on average (worst 0.75 m) by the time the
strike clip reaches full weight**, over a live match.

That is an observation, not arithmetic. An earlier version of this diagnostic
multiplied the table's `fade_in` by the launch speed and printed the product —
a constant dressed up as a measurement, which could not have detected the
animation failing to fire at all. It now records where the ball was when it
left and closes the record on the frame the tree reports the clip at full
weight, so it includes every real source of latency.

## 5. One contact, one animation (section 8)

The animation layer **subscribes** to the v0.9.1 signals rather than having
calls added at the emit sites. That rule — "the simulation emits and never
reads" — is what keeps animation from becoming a second physics system, and it
only holds if the physics code stays unaware of who is listening.

The `AnimationNodeOneShot` makes "one contact, one animation" true by
construction: it fires on request and takes itself off when the clip ends, so
there is no bookkeeping to get out of step. Asserted both ways — that one PASS
contact fires exactly one animation, and that the body is released afterwards.

**Plain dribble knock-ons deliberately get no clip.** They fire every 0.3–0.5 s
while carrying, and the shortest usable touch clip runs 0.45 s, so a full-body
clip per knock-on would replace the run cycle almost continuously — the carrier
would stop looking like a person running and start looking like a person
repeatedly kneeing the air. TURN and STOP are rarer and distinct, and do get
their own clip. This is a decision, not an omission, and the suite asserts it.

## 6. Two of my own errors, and what caught them

### A sign error that looked like a finding

Measuring which way the retargeted rest pose faces, I derived forward from the
shoulder line as `up × left`. In a right-handed Y-up system `right = forward ×
up`, so `left = up × forward` and therefore `forward = left × up` — the
opposite. Every character *and* the pack reported facing 180° backwards, and
the consistency across all twelve made it look like a real discovery about
retargeting rather than my own arithmetic.

Corrected: everything faces the model's local +Z, which is already this game's
forward, and `MODEL_YAW_OFFSET_DEGREES` is **0**. Unchanged in value from
before the milestone — but now a measurement rather than an assumption.

The same handedness question had a real consequence. The character's own right
is the model's **−X**, not +X, so feeding the blend space +X would play the
strafe-*left* clip whenever a player moved right — legs crossing the wrong way
on every sideways step, permanently. Two pre-existing helpers (which foot a
touch reads as, which way a keeper dives) had the same inversion; both are
animation-facing only and are now corrected via one shared `player_right()`.

### A scale regression the old suite caught

Turning on `retarget/rest_fixer/apply_node_transforms` rebaked where the rigs'
transforms live, and `AnimationController._measure_height()` measured skinned
meshes by their node transform — which says almost nothing about a skinned
mesh, because its vertices go through the skin's bind pose. That bind pose
carries a 0.01 scale here: the mesh resources measure ~21 units and render at
~0.21. The height came back a hundred times too large, and the auto-fit divides
by it, so **every character would have spawned about 1.6 cm tall.**

The v0.9.2 suite would not have caught it — it never checked size. The
pre-existing `CharacterPipelineTest` did, on its bind-pose height check. The
rendered playtest now reports rendered height too: **1.600 m to 1.600 m against
a 1.60 m target.**

## 7. Performance (section 20)

22 animated characters, measured against the same match with the library
switched off, using the engine's process + physics timers:

```
animation OFF (procedural fallback)   mean 3.96ms   worst 5.05ms
animation ON  (22 AnimationTrees)     mean 6.64ms   worst 10.76ms
                                      +2.69ms mean (+68%), worst +5.7ms
```

Library build: 38 clips processed once, shared by all 22 players, **147–170 ms**
at startup.

**This is CPU only.** It runs headless, so it measures the AnimationTree and
skeleton work and not the GPU cost of drawing eleven skinned characters, which
a phone also pays. 6.6 ms of a 16.6 ms budget is comfortable on this desktop
CPU; a mid-range phone is commonly 3–5× slower single-threaded, which would put
animation alone near the frame budget.

**No LOD was added.** Section 21 says LOD only if profiling proves need, and
the profiling I can do here does not — it proves a desktop cost and lets me
extrapolate a mobile risk. Designing a specific LOD scheme against an
extrapolation is how you get the wrong scheme. The measurement to take next is
on a device.

## 8. Fallback (section 24)

`AnimationLibraryCache.force_disabled` makes the pack's absence testable rather
than hypothetical. With it set: nothing claims to be animated, no AnimationTree
is built, the T-pose arm fix poses the arms instead, action intents still
resolve to the procedural pulse, and the match plays on normally. All asserted.

## 9. Validation

| Suite | Result |
|---|---|
| `V0_9_2AnimationTest` (new) | **39 / 39** |
| `V0_9_1_1GoalkeeperTest` | 13 / 13 |
| `V0_9_1_1DecisionTest` | 17 / 17 |
| `V0_9_1GameplayCleanupTest` | 47 / 47 |
| `V0_9_0FootballFoundationTest` | 34 / 34 |
| `V0_8_8PossessionValidityTest` | all pass |
| `CharacterPipelineTest` | all pass (after the height fix and one obsolete assertion updated) |

The new suite was written against the way the v0.9.1 suite failed: it asserted
"a ball struck at 25 m/s does not launch the player it hits" and passed
*because* the ball had stopped colliding with anyone at all. A check that can be
satisfied by the feature being absent is worse than no check. So nothing here
asserts an animation "works" — each check names a specific way this milestone
could be silently broken and fails only when that thing is true. Two examples
of the discipline in practice: the OneShot check polls for the clip becoming
*active* and not only for it later stopping, because "the clip finished" is
satisfied just as well by a clip that never started; and one check asserts
every one of the 54 pack clips is either mapped or documented as unused, so a
pack update cannot silently add clips nobody looked at.

One obsolete assertion was updated rather than deleted: `CharacterPipelineTest`
demanded the T-pose fix had run, which was correct when no model had a single
clip. It now requires that the character is *either* driven by clips *or* had
the fix applied — the thing that actually has to stay true.

## 10. What is still wrong, reported not hidden

**Sprinting slides by 28%.** One gait in the pack; a rate-scaled jog is not a
sprint. 3% of match samples.

**Per-direction rate error of ±13%.** One playback rate serves the whole blend
space while the eight clips' natural speeds span 2.35–3.01 m/s, so a player
running sideways slides a little more than one running straight.

**Strike clips are ~85% wind-up that is never seen.** The pack has no clip
authored for a game that launches the ball without a telegraph. The 0.43 m lag
is the cost.

**20 mapped clips are unreachable** for want of gameplay states: headers,
volleys, throw-ins, getting up, and most of the keeper set.

**The backpedal and strafe clips are almost never asked for** — 3% of samples
combined — because players turn to face where they are going.

**Mobile performance is extrapolated, not measured.** +2.7 ms CPU on desktop.

**One cross-check disagrees and I could not resolve it.** Measuring travel
direction from the planted foot instead of the hips gives a consistent ~23°
skew (the lateral wobble of footfalls) and, on `jog backward diagonal (2)`
only, a result 178° from the authoritative pack-space measurement. The
pack-space figures are used because they form a clean mirror-consistent
rosette; the foot-derived method is kept in the diagnostic as a cross-check
that is right on seven clips out of eight and wrong on one.

## Honest summary

The 54 clips are inventoried, measured and classified; 38 are mapped with
reasons recorded for the 16 that are not; 18 are reachable in a running match
and every one of those is covered by a test that fails if it breaks. Gameplay
is untouched and every prior suite is still green. Characters render at the
right size, face the right way, and run with real stride on three different
rigs checked visually.

The animation layer is a layer: it reads the simulation's events and never
writes to it, its root motion is stripped at build time, and it can be switched
off and the game still plays.

What it is not is finished. Sprinting slides, most of the goalkeeper set has
nothing to trigger it, and the mobile budget is an extrapolation. Those are
named above rather than smoothed over.
