# v0.9.2.1 — Animation QA Fix + Physical Challenge Pass

Six QA findings, worked in the order the brief set them. Five are fixed and
measured. The first — the recurring visual artifact — produced a real,
objectively wrong defect that is now fixed, but **I could not demonstrate that
it is the artifact you saw**, and that is stated plainly below rather than
smoothed over.

---

## 1. The visual artifact — a real defect found, causation NOT established

### What I ruled out, by measurement

**Not a flung bone, and not root motion.** `tests/diag_artifact_hunt.gd`
watches every bone of every animated player, every physics frame, for 2400
frames, against each rig's own rest extent. Result: **no bone ever left its
envelope**, and the hips vertical channel peaked at 0.63× rest — well inside
normal. My leading hypothesis going in was that v0.9.2's mis-scaled position
tracks had survived into the vertical channel; that is falsified.

### What I did find

Dumping every drawable in a live match by world size:

```
197.79m  Players/@CharacterBody3D@21/Model/AnimationController/...  dist 10.11
197.79m  Players/@CharacterBody3D@43/...                            dist 14.65
192.02m  Players/@CharacterBody3D@7/...                             dist 11.78
```

**Every 1.6 m character was telling the renderer it occupied a ~190 metre
volume.**

Root cause, measured end to end: a skinned mesh's vertices reach their final
position through the skin — `world = bone_global_pose * bind_pose * vertex` —
and on these rigs the mesh resources are authored ~21 units tall with a
**0.01 bind-pose scale** that brings them back to metres. An AABB is never
skinned. Godot bounds a MeshInstance3D by `mesh.get_aabb()` through the node
transform, which does not include the bind pose, so the bounds come out 100×
too large — and the 7.38× height normalisation multiplies that again.

It appeared in v0.9.2 because that milestone turned on
`retarget/rest_fixer/apply_node_transforms`, which rebaked the meshes into
that 21-unit space. Before it they were already in metres and the accidental
agreement held.

Consequences are real: nothing is ever frustum-culled, and the directional
light's shadow fitting works from those bounds.

**Fixed** in `AnimationController._fix_render_bounds()` by stating the real
bounds — the skeleton's own rest extent grown by 30% — as an explicit
`custom_aabb`. Measured **192 m → 3.58 m**, asserted in the suite. Nothing is
hidden; the renderer is told the truth.

### Why I am not claiming this fixes what you saw

I set up a controlled A/B — same seed, same scripted camera, one run with the
old bounds and one with the new — and measured the fraction of each frame
below a luminance of 0.20 (chosen after sampling the artifact itself: it is a
dark *blue*-black at rgb 0.024/0.071/0.129, which a per-channel test misses on
the blue channel alone).

```
MEAN dark fraction   legacy 0.0127   fixed 0.0232
```

The fixed build has **more** dark pixels, not fewer. And the paired
screenshots at the same sample index show **different match states**, so the
seed did not make the runs deterministic and the comparison is not controlled
after all.

Earlier I compared two screenshots from different runs and concluded the black
wedges were gone. That was not evidence — the runs diverge, the camera is
elsewhere, and the numbers move for unrelated reasons.

So: a genuine and serious rendering defect is fixed and proven fixed. Whether
it was *your* artifact is **unknown**. What would settle it is a build in your
hands. If the obstruction is still there, the next candidates in order are the
directional light's shadow configuration (`shadow_bias 0.10`,
`normal_bias 2.0`, `pancake_size 20`, 4 splits over 100 m — all untouched by
this pass) and the stadium stand geometry, which is the only thing in the
scene big enough to cast something that large.

## 2 & 3. Role separation, enforced structurally

The v0.9.2 lookup checked the outfield table and then the keeper table, so any
keeper intent was reachable by any player. Nothing was calling for one yet —
which is why I could not reproduce your report by reading the code — but the
structure permitted it, and the keeper set was about to grow from 4 clips to
22.

`AnimationSet` is now a clip database where every intent declares a **Role**
and a **Category**, and `AnimationController.play_action()` **refuses** an
intent belonging to the other role. A refusal is counted and warned, not
silently swapped for something else, so a leak fails a test instead of showing
up as a striker diving.

Asserted three ways: as a property of the data (no keeper intent is selectable
by an outfield role, and vice versa), through the controller (a striker asked
for **all 13** keeper intents plays none, and the 13 refusals are counted), and
over a live match (**0 refusals**, no outfield player ending on a keeper clip).

The live-match check found one real leak immediately: a **keeper** winning the
ball called `play_action("tackle")`, an outfield intent. Fixed.

It also found a classification error of mine. `tripped` / `fallen` / `get_up`
were marked OUTFIELD, so a fouled *keeper* was refused and stayed upright
through a foul. Going down and getting up are things a body does, not skills a
position has: they are now `Role.ANY`.

## 5–10. The slide tackle as a physical event

New `scripts/SlideTackle.gd`. A slide is a **committed action with a
duration**, and its outcome is decided every frame by measured geometry
between three bodies. The animation is triggered *by* the outcome and never
decides it — asserted directly: playing the tackle clip on its own resolves no
tackle, puts nobody into a slide, and knocks nobody over.

**Geometry, not centre distance.** A sliding player's reach is the *segment*
from their body centre to a leading foot 1.25 m ahead, and the ball is
measured against that whole line (`_point_to_segment`). Bodies are in contact
at 0.92 m — two 0.40 capsules plus a frame of overlap margin.

**Four outcomes, all reachable, all observed in a live match:**

| outcome | condition |
|---|---|
| CLEAN | the leg reached the ball; the ball is knocked and possession may transfer |
| FOUL | significant body contact **without** having played the ball first |
| MISSED | ran its course touching neither |
| AVOIDED | missed, and the carrier had cut away after the commitment |

Over 60 s of live play: `CLEAN 5, MISSED 1`, and in another run
`CLEAN 3, MISSED 4, FOUL 1`. Slides are not free wins.

**Commitment is what makes avoidance possible** (section 10). The direction is
locked at `begin_slide` and never revisited, so a carrier who cuts away is
gone — asserted: a committed slide cannot re-aim, and the carrier keeps the
ball.

**Foul foundation** (section 8): a `fouled` signal and a `slide_resolved`
signal carrying `CLEAN_TACKLE / MISSED_TACKLE / FOUL_CONTACT / AVOIDED`. The
carrier goes down and gets back up. **A referee, free kicks and cards are
deliberately not built** — the brief said not to redesign the match system, so
the event is emitted and logged and stops there.

**Reactions** (section 9) use authored clips: `soccer trip` going down,
`fallen idle` while down, `standing up` on recovery. Nothing freezes: the
timers are counted down by the player itself, not by an external system.
That was a real bug — with the countdown living in `SlideTackle.update`, a
knocked-over player only got up while some other system happened to be
running, and the test caught it as a player still down 1.3 s later.

### Two bugs found by measurement rather than by reading

**Closing speed was read after the collision.** `move_and_slide` overwrites
`velocity` with what the body *achieved*, which is near zero on the frame two
capsules collide — exactly when a challenge is hardest. Every hard slide
therefore measured as gentle, and the foul scenario resolved as MISSED with the
bodies visibly touching. Now read from the slide's committed momentum. (v0.9.1
documented this same trap for the ball shove; I walked into it again.)

**The commit rule never fired.** The first cut required accumulated challenge
progress, on the theory that a slide is the end of a duel. `diag_slide_window`
measured 2600+ defender-frames per match:

```
gap <= 3.2m              44.6%      progress >= 0.52          1.4%   <-- binding
speed >= 3.4 m/s         62.4%      ball outside poke reach  83.4%
approach >= 0.35         48.0%      ALL AT ONCE               0.0%
```

Progress is at or near zero on 75% of those frames. The gate was also the
wrong idea: a slide is a defender closing fast on a carrier they cannot reach
by pressing, not the culmination of a long contest. It was removed and paid
for with a much stricter lunge — nearly straight at the carrier (0.65 against
0.35) at real speed (4.0 against 3.4), with a minimum range as well as a
maximum. Fires on 9.6% of defender-frames; the per-player cooldown does the
rest.

in a slide or a stumble, and none left the world.

## What is still open

**The reported visual artifact is not confirmed fixed.** See section 1. A real
190 m bounds defect was found and fixed; the causal link to what you saw is
unproven, and the honest next step is your eyes on this build.

**Fouls emit an event and nothing else.** No referee, no free kick, no cards —
deliberately, per the scope limit.

**`goalkeeper overhand throw` is mapped but unreachable.** Keeper distribution
currently routes through the clear (kick) and the pass; there is no gameplay
state that means "throw it short".

**Sprinting still slides 28% at the rate ceiling**, unchanged from v0.9.2 — the
pack has one gait and no sprint clip.

**Mobile performance is still an extrapolation** (+2.7 ms CPU on desktop for
22 animated characters). Unchanged by this pass.
